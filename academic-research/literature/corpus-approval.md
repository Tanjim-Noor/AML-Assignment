# Candidate Literature Corpus for Approval

## Decision requested

Approve candidate corpus `aml-report-corpus-v1` for transfer to the UARS writing workflow.

## Corpus composition

The corpus contains 15 DOI-verified scholarly sources:

- six sources on generative-AI opportunities, outcomes and risks;
- three sources on AI literacy and prompt engineering;
- five sources on academic-performance prediction, validation and model choice;
- one foundational systematic review of AI in higher education.

Publication years range from 2018 to 2026. The set includes systematic and scoping reviews, a meta-analysis, primary observational and quasi-experimental studies, an educational-data experiment, and methodological guidance. It intentionally includes positive, negative and null/qualified findings.

## Evidence the corpus can support

1. AI applications in higher education include prediction, assessment, assistance and personalisation.
2. Evidence about GenAI and academic performance is mixed and depends on design, context and manner of use.
3. Prompting and AI literacy are multidimensional skills, but robust measurement remains immature.
4. Student-performance prediction studies use diverse targets, predictors and validation practices, limiting direct score comparison.
5. Prior achievement and behavioural/contextual variables are repeatedly useful, but intervention claims require stronger designs than predictive models.
6. Rigorous reporting, leakage control, cross-validation and explicit tuning are necessary for defensible model comparison.

## Evidence the corpus cannot support

- A claim that AI use causes GPA improvement or decline in the selected dataset.
- Population estimates for Malaysian, APU or global students.
- A peer-reviewed benchmark using the exact Kaggle dataset.
- Direct metric equivalence between classification accuracy and GPA-change regression error.

## Search limitations

- OpenAlex anonymous search was unavailable because of an API-key/rate-limit response.
- Semantic Scholar's anonymous Graph API returned HTTP 429; public Semantic Scholar result pages were used for discovery instead.
- Crossref's broad bibliographic search was low precision, so all admitted records underwent targeted DOI/title verification.
- The Kaggle dataset metadata was public, but its notebook-listing API returned HTTP 401. No public same-dataset analysis with inspectable methods and results was located.
- The review is structured and reproducible but does not claim full PRISMA systematic-review status.

## Proposed synthesis structure

1. Evolution of AI and GenAI in higher education.
2. Mixed evidence on AI use and academic outcomes.
3. AI literacy, prompt skill and quality of use.
4. Predictive modelling of academic performance.
5. Validation, leakage and interpretability.
6. Research gap and relationship to notebook 05.

Approval authorises synthesis and drafting from these sources. It does not authorise stronger claims than the evidence supports.
